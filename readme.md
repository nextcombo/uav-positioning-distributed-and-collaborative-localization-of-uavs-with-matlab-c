# UAV Positioning: Distributed and Collaborative Localization of UAVs [with MATLAB Code]

In recent years, unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), especially swarm UAVs, have been widely deployed in various Internet of Things (IoT) scenarios. Due to the critical role of UAV positioning for their collaboration, high-precision positioning of swarm UAVs has attracted widespread attention. Although global positioning system (GPS) receivers have been extensively integrated into UAVs, their accuracy is not sufficient and can easily be interfered with by accidental or intentional disturbances. In this paper, we propose a distributed and collaborative swarm UAV positioning method that combines Super Multidimensional Scaling (SMDS) with patch partitioning/merging and GPS information. Specifically, firstly, SMDS is used to obtain the relative coordinates of each drone within each patch, then the relative map patches are merged into a global map, and the relative coordinates of the drones are converted into their absolute coordinates. Additionally, we propose a low-complexity algorithm that significantly reduces the computational complexity of SMDS for a large number of drones. Simulation results verify that the proposed SMDS positioning algorithm outperforms other collaborative positioning algorithms based on MDS when angle measurements are accurate enough, significantly improving the positioning accuracy and robustness of swarm UAVs.
Due to the advantages of drones (UAVs) in terms of maneuverability and cost, they are increasingly being applied in many Internet of Things (IoT) scenarios [1]–[4]. A UAV can accomplish many tasks that a single UAV cannot. In terms of UAV swarm systems, there are mainly four building blocks: 1) Control; 2) Sensing; 3) Coordination; 4) Connection [5]. All these building blocks rely on the network established between vehicles and/or ground stations. For example, control requires communication between the ground station and the UAV to influence its behavior, while coordination requires communication between UAVs to achieve local decisions, cooperation, self-organization, and avoidance of collisions. For swarmed UAVs, their position is crucial for various forms of collaboration, including navigation, motion control, and task completion [6]–[9]. There are various positioning methods, among which Global Positioning System (GPS) is most widely used due to its global coverage, convenience, and flexibility [8]–[10]. Although GPS provides an attractive solution, it also has some limitations that hinder its application [6], [11]. As a line-of-sight (LoS) technology, GPS signals become very weak or distorted in shadow areas such as tunnels and dense vegetation. For swarmed UAVs, only GPS-based positioning could be catastrophic.
In adverse environmental conditions where geographic positioning fails, communication between UAVs can improve the accuracy of positioning [12]. As a promising technology, cellular connectivity UAVs [13] and [14] have achieved high-speed, low-latency, and ultra-reliable wireless communication between UAVs and their associated ground users. Good communication conditions provide the possibility of collaborative positioning for UAV swarms.
The main idea of collaborative methods is to obtain information about some bee-like drones, such as the distance between them shown in Figure 1, so that positioning information can be corrected from GPS. Fortunately, with the development of ultra-wideband (UWB) technology in the field of wireless sensor networks (WSN) [15]–[17], and assuming LoS conditions exist among drones [18], it is possible to perform high-precision ranging based on time-of-arrival (ToA) at a low cost even in dense multipath environments.
The following are numerous collaborative localization methods based on Multidimensional Scaling (MDS) [19]–[25]. MDS is a dimensionality reduction technique originating from mathematical psychology, which has been used to obtain relative coordinates of objects based on their similarity/dissimilarity. However, MDS requires the complete pairwise Euclidean distances of UAVs, which poses a challenge for cluster connectivity and results in a low tolerance for missing or incorrectly estimated measurement values [11]. Moreover, computing an N×N kernel matrix N for UAVs introduces significant computational complexity N. To address these issues with MDS, several improved algorithms have been proposed, such as MDS-MAP, MDS-MAP(P), and Super MDS (SMDS). The MDS-MAP proposed in [20] first utilizes the Floyd algorithm to roughly complement the distances between unconnected node pairs with their multi-hop distances, then applies MDS to the complementary distance matrix. However, the introduction of the Floyd algorithm not only increases computational complexity but also leads to larger localization errors, especially in large-scale networks with irregular node distributions. The MDS-MAP(P) proposed in [21] divides the entire network into multiple patches, applies MDS-MAP in each patch, and then merges them to reduce hop counts, thereby reducing distance errors. Additionally, MDS-MAP(P) is a distributed localization algorithm suitable for large-scale networks meeting the required number of common nodes. However, due to the error propagation effect caused by the positioning errors of common nodes, the localization error of MDS-MAP(P) increases with the number of merging times. Therefore, how to reduce the number of merging times is crucial for localized methods in distributed collaboration. To mitigate the error propagation effect, [21] proposed MDS-MAP(P,R), which refines the relative mapping calculated by MDS-MAP(P) through iterative optimization methods, improving localization accuracy but significantly increasing computational complexity. Since iterative algorithms are not suitable for real-time localization tasks, non-iterative SMDS methods [11] were developed to improve localization precision by utilizing additional arrival angle (AoA) information between node pairs. Unfortunately, this approach leads to extremely high computational complexity O(N6). Subsequently, in SMDS [24], Nyström approximation [26] was introduced to some extent to reduce computational complexity, but the complexity of SMDS remains too high for large-scale clusters with many UAVs.
We aim to improve the SMDS method, significantly reducing its complexity and making it applicable to large-scale clustered UAV networks. To this end, we propose a two-stage distributed positioning approach called SMDS (P), which integrates SMDS and patch split/merge with GPS information. Specifically, SMDS is used to obtain the relative coordinates of UAVs in each patch. The Nyström approximation and power method are employed to reduce the computational complexity of SMDS. Subsequently, Procrustes analysis is conducted to merge the relative map patches into a global map, and the relative coordinates of the drones are converted into their absolute coordinates. During the merging of the relative mapping, coordinate transformation requires sufficient common nodes (three for 2-D positioning and four for 3-D positioning) [21]. However, considering the sparse connection situation of clustered UAVs, there is generally not enough common nodes available for merging. Therefore, based on the number of common nodes with adjacent patches, we divide the drone patches in the swarm into I-type patches and II-type patches. Then, a virtual common node method is proposed for II-type patches. Since the positioning error of the SMDS common nodes is less than that of MDS-MAP common nodes, as long as the AoA estimation is accurate enough, the error propagation effect of patch merging in the proposed SMDS (P) algorithm is less than that in MDS-MAP (P). Simulation results show that the proposed SMDS (P)-Ny-PM algorithm outperforms other MDS-based collaborative positioning algorithms, significantly improving the accuracy and robustness of GPS positioning for clustered UAVs. Conclusion
This paper proposes a distributed collaborative UAV 3D positioning method based on SMDS (P) and its low complexity algorithm SMDS (P)-Ny-PM, which can improve the positioning accuracy and robustness of UAVs solely relying on GPS. Compared with the MDS (M)-based algorithms that only measure range (such as MDS-MAP and MDS-MAP (P)), SMDS (P)-Ny-PM enhances positioning accuracy and reduces the computational complexity from \mathcal {O} (N^6) to \mathcal {O} (nk^3). Simulation results show that the positioning accuracy of SMDS-Ny and SMDS (P)-Ny-PM is less sensitive to communication range, significantly outperforming MDS-MAP and MDS-MAP (P). Moreover, under accurate AoA estimation, the proposed SMDS (P)-Ny-PM algorithm outperforms other MDS-based collaborative positioning algorithms, greatly improving the positioning accuracy and robustness of clustered UAVs reliant on GPS.
Although in this paper, we consider that the positional accuracy obtained from available GPS signals is normal, in actual shadow areas, even these available GPS signals may become very faint or highly distorted, or outdated in high dynamic scenarios. Therefore, to further improve the positioning accuracy and robustness of clustered UAVs, other common sensors such as gyroscopes and accelerometers can be involved. Then, Bayesian, Kalman, and particle filters can be combined with the proposed collaborative positioning method for clustered UAVs to achieve better performance, which will be left for future work.
